# Culture and Generations — Learned, Never Authored

Nathan's constraint, verbatim: culture should differ by environment and by the overseer's choices, and none of it should be hard-coded. The resolution is the same one [knowledge](22-knowledge-and-discovery.md) used for maps: **model the substrate, never the content**. We build the *capacity to have traditions* — where they live, how they form, how they die — and what any people actually comes to believe is left entirely to its history.

## 1. Culture is memory of what worked

Per nation: a small vector of **learned priors** — no types, no labels, no "culture kinds". Each prior is a slow-moving weight updated only by lived outcomes:

- **Subsistence leanings**: months where a channel actually fed the people nudge its prior up (a slow EMA over realized nutrition by source). The autopilot's return-following gains a tradition term: a people that fished for three generations *hesitates* at the plow even when the marginal table favors it — tradition as honest inertia, born from the landscape, wrong exactly as often as real traditions are.
- **Mobility and daring**: blind moves that saved the band raise the wandering prior; scouts that never came back lower the scouting one. A people's willingness to gamble is its own scar tissue.
- **Command and self-rule**: the mandate/autonomy loop ([16](16-mandate-and-works.md)) already is this — repeated decrees teach a people to be governed or to resent it. Overseer choices thereby shape culture through the same door as everything else: outcomes.

Reports render priors as plain numbers with mechanism-honest phrasing ("the people lean to the waters: 62%"), never identity labels ([19 §4](19-ecology-and-subsistence.md) anti-teleology holds — nobody is "a fishing culture"; they have a history with the water). Two nations of the same generated people, planted in different valleys under different overseers, drift into different priors — environment and governance, exactly the two forces asked for, and nothing else.

## 2. Generations are the substrate

Age-structured cohorts (children / adults / elders per tile-species) are what makes culture *inhabit people* rather than float as nation-state:

- **Work and need by stage**: adults carry the labor; children and elders eat without extracting. The dependency ratio becomes a real, visible number, and a famine's missing children return as a missing workforce twenty years on — demographic echo, unauthored.
- **Priors live in the old**: cultural weights are carried disproportionately by elders. A famine or a war that takes a generation of elders *erases tradition* — the priors decay toward neutral when their carriers die. Oral culture loss emerges from mortality tables, the exact mechanic by which real traditions vanish, and later a grammar-invented writing technology can slow it ([22]'s forgetting hook, same slot).
- **Levies and echoes (later)**: war draws adults; a bled generation echoes like a famine's. The substrate is shared with everything downstream.

## 3. What is deliberately NOT built

No authored culture traits, no civ-style bonuses, no belief systems, no value taxonomies. If a future feature wants "a warrior culture" or "a seafaring people", the answer is: that phrase must only ever be *true as a description* of priors some nation earned — never a type the generator can roll or a modifier anyone can pick. The [doc-21](21-authored-floor.md) tests hold: every prior changes decisions (autopilot term, report line), every echo is watchable (the thin generation in the population table), and every outcome narrates in one sentence ("the elders who remembered the herds died in the hunger, and the young returned to the river").

**Ships as:** age-banded cohorts with derived work/need/mortality weights; the learned-prior vector with outcome-driven updates; the tradition term in autopilot allocation; report lines for dependency and leanings; elder-carried decay. **Deferred, slots ready:** transmission between nations (contact spreads priors — by the knowledge channel, at the speed of people); writing as decay-brake; priors as diplomacy-visible character ("they are known to wander").
